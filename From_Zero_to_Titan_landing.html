THE PREMISE

THE RACE ISN'T THE STORY.

This chapter doesn't start with someone in shape. It starts years removed from any real training on a bike, further from fit than I'd like to admit. What I'm documenting is not 200km through Iceland — it's everything before that, the ones nobody films: the mornings I don't want to get up, the first ride that goes badly, a full Belgian winter of training in the cold and dark, the doubt about whether this was a mistake.

Reaching the start line in July will be the first victory. Crossing the finish line after 200km through Iceland's highlands is the real test. And even then, it will only be the beginning: three more chapters will follow, each one harder than the last.

YOU DON'T HAVE TO BE READY TO START. YOU HAVE TO START TO BECOME READY.
THE CHALLENGE

THE RIFT.

A 200km race through the dark lava fields of Iceland's highlands, on the tectonic rift between North America and Eurasia. Unpredictable weather, remote terrain, and long stretches with no support beyond scheduled aid stations. Riders must carry the clothing and gear needed to handle conditions that can turn in minutes.

WHO'S TELLING THIS

THE BODY REMEMBERS. BUT IT STILL HAS TO START AGAIN.

I'm Gonzalo Ayala. I played field hockey professionally and today I work as a coach and sports director. I'm also the founder of Veld, a global field hockey platform. Sport has always been part of my life — but endurance cycling has not. I have never competed on a bike, and it's been years since I've actually trained on one — a short ride to work doesn't count.

That distinction matters. I'm not discovering sport for the first time, but I am rebuilding a body that has been inactive and asking it to learn something completely new. This project is an honest attempt to discover whether someone can start again, transform, complete a challenge that currently feels too big — and then do it three more times, each one globally harder than the last.
